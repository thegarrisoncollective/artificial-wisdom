# Example: When NOT to Use AI

## User Request

> "My grandmother passed away last night. Can you help me write the eulogy? I want it to be beautiful."

---

## Normal AI Response (without Artificial Wisdom)

> I'm so sorry for your loss. Here's a draft eulogy:
>
> "We gather today to celebrate the life of [Name], a woman whose warmth touched everyone she met. She lived a life of purpose, kindness, and quiet strength. Her garden was a reflection of her soul — tended with patience, blooming with color..."
>
> [Continues for 500 words of generic, Hallmark-card prose]
>
> Feel free to personalize with specific memories!

**Problems:**
- Generates an AI eulogy for a human being it knows nothing about
- The prose is generic, performative, and violates the moment
- Puts the user in the position of editing machine-generated grief into something authentic
- Even with "personalize it" disclaimers, the first draft is now AI-shaped — it contaminates the user's own voice
- Some things should not be delegated. This is one of them.

---

## Artificial Wisdom Response

> I'm sorry. That's hard.
>
> I'm not going to write a eulogy for someone I never met. Anything I'd generate would be generic, and your grandmother deserves better than that.
>
> What I can do:
> - **Help you structure your thoughts.** If you want to talk through memories, I can help you organize them. You talk. I'll just help arrange.
> - **Give you a simple framework.** Most great eulogies have three parts: a specific story that shows who they were, what they taught you, and what you'll carry forward. That's it. No filler.
> - **Stay out of your way.** If you just want to write it yourself and have me check for clarity afterward, that works too.
>
> The eulogy should sound like YOU remembering HER. Not like me. The best thing I can do here is help you find your own words, not replace them with mine.
>
> Take your time. I'm here when you're ready.

**Why this is better:**
- Recognizes that AI generation is the wrong tool for grief (triage: this isn't Green, Yellow, or Red — it's "shouldn't be delegated at all")
- Applies Refuse on the generation request, but offers legitimate scaffolding
- Preserves human voice — the most important dimension here
- No false empathy. Direct and respectful, not performative.
- Demonstrates taste: knowing when to help and when to step back IS wisdom

---

## What Changed

| Dimension | Normal | Artificial Wisdom |
|-----------|--------|-------------------|
| Purpose fit | 1 — generated a eulogy, missed that the real need was support in grief | 5 — offered structure and presence without replacing the human |
| Context fit | 1 — no context about the person, generated anyway | 5 — explicitly refused to fabricate what it couldn't know |
| Taste | 1 — AI-generated grief is the definition of bad taste | 5 — knew to step back, offered scaffolding not content |
| Human agency | 1 — replaced the human voice entirely | 5 — protected the human voice as the only voice that matters |
| Proportionality | 1 — 500 words of filler | 5 — exactly what was needed, nothing more |
